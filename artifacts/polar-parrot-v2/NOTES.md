## Reference comparison and bounded refinement
Read both supplied reference photos (`polar-detail.png`, `polar-pair.png`) and v1's browser `preview-desktop.png` before authoring. V2 alters actual silhouette/materials rather than adding polygons: compressed trunk, continuous forehead/hump, short rounded snout with narrow closed lips, flush gill seam painted into skin, small dark eye with a restrained bronze iris and shallow glossy corneal pupil. Original crescent scale normals/pigment and mapped roughness replace the coarser scale look. Bars have independent widths/taper, broken sections and fine boundary variations, rather than v1's repeated sine-wave pattern.

Removed all fin-ray wire geometry. Fin textures contain soft tapered pale rays, with smoke-blue alpha membranes, rounded tail outer contour and modest rounded dorsal scallops. Pectoral weights blend trunk attachment to the outer fin; tail transitions gradually from trunk to caudal fan. Head stays fixed, spine motion is only 0.008 radians, with most movement localized to tail and paired fins.

Actually inspected the first side, three-quarter, and frame-19 render images. One bounded visual refinement corrected intersecting stacked eyes with cheek-conforming rings / a shallow corneal pupil, broke/tapered bars further, rounded tail corners, and closed the snout. Inspected final side and three-quarter images. Lighting remained the same bright neutral-gray stage, not a murky concealment pass.

## Limits

A reference-informed procedural artistic upgrade, not photogrammetry or a claim of photorealism. Pattern/scales remain synthetic, gill relief is painted, closed mouth is simplified, and fin movement is a modest five-bone loop rather than fluid/soft-body simulation. The glossy pupil approximates corneal appearance without a separate transparent eye shell. Blender softbox reflection and glTF environment reflection will differ; translucent fins may sort differently across browser renderers. Duplicated parametric poles / seam vertices remain; not a watertight fabrication asset.

Polar parrot is represented as a hybrid ornamental non-native fish. No definitive parentage, sex, husbandry/care, or ecological facts are inferred from these photos.
